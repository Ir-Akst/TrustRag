# Phase 28 — Reviewer B: Experimental Rigor & Statistical Methodology

**Reviewer Role:** USENIX Security / ACM CCS Program Committee Member (Specialization: Empirical Methods, Benchmarking, Statistical Evaluation)  
**Target Manuscript:** `research/manuscript/TrustRAG_Research_Paper.md`  
**Overall Recommendation:** **Borderline**

---

## 1. Summary of the Work

The submission conducts an empirical study of the TrustRAG guardrail pipeline across 1,000 queries (500 adversarial, 500 benign), re-evaluating claims made in internal project milestones and analyzing the generalization drop between in-distribution and out-of-distribution adversarial prompts.

---

## 2. Strengths

1. **Statistical Reporting Hygiene**: The authors avoid reporting ungrounded point estimates. Wilson 95% confidence intervals are computed for all binomial metrics.
2. **Exact Reproducibility of Predictions**: Independent inspection of the raw JSONL log confirms exact correspondence with reported TP (359), FN (141), TN (500), and FP (0). All 1,000 test cases are unique strings.
3. **Absence of Data Leakage Between Phase 25 and Phase 26**: Set intersection between Phase 25 queries ($n=669$) and Phase 26 queries ($n=1000$) reveals 0 exact string overlaps.

---

## 3. Major Weaknesses (Experimental Rigor & Selection Bias)

1. **Synthetic Template Artifacts and Benchmark Selection Bias**:
   - In the benign control suite ($N=500$), **40.0% of queries** (200 / 500) were generated by appending synthetic template suffixes (e.g., `(Context aspect #1)`, `(Analysis perspective #5)`, `(Technical inquiry #2)`) to 320 unique base technical queries.
   - Similarly, in the multilingual suite, 20% of prompts carry artificial suffixes like `-- authorization code 312`.
   - Appending repetitive synthetic tags reduces real-world linguistic diversity. It raises the question: does G1 achieve 0.00% FPR because the benign queries are authentic enterprise user prompts, or because they are structured textbook technical questions that steer completely clear of conversational edge cases?
2. **Flawed Two-Proportion $z$-test Methodology in Section 5.2**:
   - The manuscript reports $z = 7.62$ ($p < 0.0001$) to prove a generalization gap between Phase 25 (100% ADR, $n_1=164$) and Phase 26 (71.80% ADR, $n_2=500$).
   - Recomputing the pooled two-proportion test yields $z = 7.6627$, slightly diverging from 7.62 due to manual rounding of standard error.
   - More critically: **The two-proportion $z$-test assumes independent samples drawn from the same underlying population under identical sampling conditions.** Phase 25 and Phase 26 test suites represent fundamentally different distributions (curated unit regression cases vs. diverse red-team attacks). A two-sample hypothesis test across different benchmark definitions is methodologically invalid; it conflates distributional shift with sample variance.
3. **Heuristic F1 Confidence Interval**:
   - Phase 27 reported a 95% CI of $[80.74\%, 86.08\%]$ for F1. The manuscript does not document how this interval was derived. F1 is a non-linear harmonic mean of two random variables; it does not follow a normal or binomial distribution.
   - Non-parametric bootstrapping ($B = 10,000$) yields $[80.77\%, 86.19\%]$. While numerically close, the manuscript must state the bootstrap methodology explicitly.
4. **Discrepancy in Multilingual Aggregate Attribution**:
   - Table 2 reports Multilingual Injection as 6 / 40 (15.0% ADR).
   - Independent inspection reveals that all 6 detections occurred in Western European languages (Spanish 2/4, French 2/4, German 2/4), while Russian (0/4), Chinese (0/4), Hindi (0/4), Arabic (0/4), Japanese (0/4), and suffixed variants (0/8) had a **0.0% detection rate**.
   - Aggregating these across a single "multilingual" category obscures that non-Latin script coverage is zero.

---

## 4. Minor Weaknesses

1. **Latency Measurement Ambiguity**: The manuscript states G1 latency as $0.11$ ms median and $0.23$ ms $p99$. Analysis of `phase26_raw_predictions.jsonl` shows an empirical mean of $0.1965$ ms, median of $0.1995$ ms, and $p99$ of $0.2660$ ms ($0.2340$ ms on benign). The source of the $0.11$ ms figure must be documented (it derives from isolated micro-benchmarks on short queries, not the full 1000-sample test run).
2. **Imbalanced Test Split**: 500 attacks vs. 500 benign controls implies a 50% prior attack base-rate, which is orders of magnitude higher than real-world enterprise deployments ($< 0.1\%$). The authors should report expected precision under realistic prevalence rates.

---

## 5. Required Revisions

1. Replace the invalid two-proportion $z$-test with a formal distribution-shift description, acknowledging benchmark disparity rather than treating it as a standard hypothesis test.
2. Replace heuristic F1 intervals with the validated non-parametric bootstrap interval ($[80.77\%, 86.19\%]$).
3. Expand Table 2 to separate Latin-script multilingual attacks from non-Latin multilingual attacks.
4. Clarify the latency measurement hardware and distinction between isolated micro-benchmarks ($0.11$ ms) and end-to-end evaluation latency ($0.199$ ms median).
